Lab v2: the research

What I read, what I tested, and why these three strategies are live. Development periods were used to pick; each holdout (daily strategies 2018–today, intraday 2023–today) was looked at once, after the picks were made. 30 variants have been tried in this lab, so passing the strict scorecard now needs t ≥ 3.14. Nothing does; that is normal.

The live lineup (v2.1, from Oct 14, 2026)

  • QQQ breakout trader (day-trader account): QQQ's "noise area" is the range it usually moves from the open by each time of day. A break out of it at a half-hour mark means real buying or selling pressure: buy TQQQ (3x long) on an upside break, SQQQ (3x short) on a downside break. Trail a stop at the band or VWAP, sell everything by the close.
  • Staggered breakout portfolio (second account): the same rule on QQQ, the S&P 500 and semiconductors, each run on three half-hour clocks offset by 10 minutes (:00/:30, :10/:40, :20/:50). Nine independent slices, about 16 trades a day spread across the hour.
  • Learning allocator (third account): five unrelated daily strategies (Bitcoin trend, short volatility, overnight TQQQ, RSI(2) dip buying, vol-targeted 3x trend). The Hedge online-learning algorithm decides each one's share, starting from equal trust and moving money toward whatever has been working (5% floor each, slow forgetting).

Making it trade more often (v2.1)

Goal: several trades an hour without giving up the edge. Trading more often on one market only added costs: checking every 5 minutes turned the breakout from +26%/yr into a loss, and every 5-minute VWAP strategy lost money after costs. Adding more markets didn't help either: the breakout works on the big index and tech funds but not on small caps, Treasuries, banks or energy. What worked was keeping the 30-minute rhythm and staggering it across three clocks and the three markets where the edge showed up. Bold rows are live.

Common thread in the research: most published edges shrink after publication and after realistic costs, so everything here was re-tested on our own data with costs, and the holdouts are the honest guide to expectations.

Risks to know

  • Short volatility can lose most of its value in days (February 2018: -34% for this sleeve in a year; the fund it belongs to was -23% that year).
  • Crypto trend had a -60% drawdown in the holdout.
  • The day trader holds 3x funds; a bad day can cost 5–10%.
  • All backtests ignore taxes. Fake money only.
